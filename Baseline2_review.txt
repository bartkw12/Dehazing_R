# 1) What Baseline 2 is trying to solve

The paper starts from the same problem setting as Baseline 1: **non-homogeneous dehazing**, where haze is spatially irregular, dense in some regions, and much harder than standard homogeneous haze handled by the atmospheric scattering model (ASM). The authors argue that while deep learning has clearly outperformed classical ASM-based approaches for this task, existing methods still struggle because **high-resolution hazy images are expensive to process**, and common architectures each have limitations: **CNNs** often rely on large fixed kernels and strong inductive bias, while **transformers** capture long-range dependencies but are often too memory/computation heavy for very large images like **4000×6000**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

So the motivation of Baseline 2 is:

*   keep strong global/contextual modeling,
*   keep adaptive handling of complex haze patterns,
*   **improve efficiency for high-resolution images**,
*   and still preserve/recover frequency/detail information and color fidelity. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

That is already the first major conceptual difference from Baseline 1: **Baseline 1 is mainly solving the representation + small-data problem** using **DWT + FFC + pretrained ConvNeXt**, while **Baseline 2 is more explicitly solving the “how do we model long-range structure efficiently on very high-res images?” problem** using **DCNv4-based transformer-like blocks**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf), [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

# 2) Core architecture of Baseline 2

The overall model in Baseline 2 is composed of **two modules**, not just a single two-branch generator as in Baseline 1:

1.  **Dehazing module**
2.  **Refinement module** [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

Inside the **Dehazing module**, there are again **two branches**:

*   a **main transformer-like dehazing branch** built from **DCNFormer blocks**, and
*   a **frequency-aware branch** (borrowed conceptually from prior frequency-aware / DWT-FFC style designs). [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

Then, after preliminary dehazing, the output is passed to a separate **lightweight Retinex-inspired transformer refinement module** that improves **color correction** and **structure/detail refinement**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

So if I compress Baseline 2 into one sentence:

> **Baseline 2 does dehazing in two stages: first remove haze with a DCNv4-based transformer-like + frequency-aware dehazing module, then refine colors/details with a lightweight Retinex-inspired transformer.** [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

# 3) What is the key technical idea of Baseline 2?

The key new idea is the **DCNFormer block**. Instead of using:

*   standard convolution only, or
*   full self-attention as in a normal transformer,

the paper uses **Deformable Convolution v4 (DCNv4)** as the core operator inside a **transformer-like block**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

The paper’s argument is that DCNv4 gives them two things at once:

### (a) **Long-range dependency**

Like transformers, they want the model to capture information beyond a tiny local window. For haze, this matters because haze is not purely local — recovering one region may depend on scene-wide context, illumination, and global degradation patterns. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf), [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

### (b) **Adaptive spatial aggregation**

Unlike fixed-grid convolution, deformable convolution learns **offsets** for where to sample features. That means the network can adaptively focus on the spatial structures that matter for a given input image/region, which is useful for **irregular haze patterns**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

The authors specifically emphasize that **traditional CNNs with large fixed kernels** can be powerful but are computationally expensive and less adaptive, while **standard self-attention** is flexible but has quadratic cost in resolution. Their solution is to replace attention with **DCNv4**, which they present as a more efficient route to transformer-like behavior on high-res dehazing. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

# 4) What exactly is a DCNFormer block?

The DCNFormer block is designed to resemble a transformer block structurally, but instead of self-attention as the core mixing operation, it uses **DCNv4**. The paper says the block includes normalization and a feed-forward network like transformer-style designs, but its central feature interaction is done by deformable convolution. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

More specifically, deformable convolution learns:

*   **offsets** $$\Delta p$$ that shift the sampling points away from the regular convolution grid, and
*   **modulation scalars** $$m$$ that control the importance of those sampled features. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

The paper also highlights a very specific change from older deformable convolution variants: **DCNv4 removes the softmax normalization on the modulation scalars** that was used in DCNv3. According to the paper, this gives:

*   **unbounded dynamic weights**,
*   **faster convergence**, and
*   **faster forward speed**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

This is a major point because it explains why Baseline 2 calls itself a **Deformable Convolutional Transformer** rather than a standard transformer. It wants the **benefits of adaptive, long-range modeling**, but with **better efficiency for restoration on large images**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

# 5) Role of the frequency-aware branch

Baseline 2 does **not** abandon frequency information. In fact, it explicitly says it keeps a **frequency-aware branch** as an auxiliary branch in the dehazing module. The figure and text indicate that this branch is related to the earlier **DWT-FFC frequency branch** idea from Baseline 1 / related work. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf), [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

That means the authors are **not rejecting** the Baseline 1 insight that frequency-domain information matters. They are effectively saying:

> “We still want frequency representations because they help with structure and restoration, but we want a stronger / more efficient main branch for spatial-context modeling.” [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf), [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

This is important for your comparison answer: **Baseline 2 is an evolution, not a total replacement of Baseline 1’s philosophy**. It preserves the belief that frequency-aware restoration helps, but changes the **primary representation engine** and adds a **new refinement stage**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf), [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

# 6) What is the refinement module doing?

This is another big architectural change.

Baseline 1 produces the final result through **fusion of the DWT-FFC branch and the ConvNeXt prior branch**, supervised with a GAN discriminator. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

Baseline 2 instead does:

1.  **preliminary dehazing** via the dehazing module,
2.  **then refinement** via a **lightweight Retinex-inspired transformer**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

The paper says this refinement module is used for:

*   **color correction**, and
*   **structure/detail refinement**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

It is inspired by **Retinex theory**, which is often used in enhancement tasks because it separates illumination-like effects from reflectance/content-like structure. In the paper’s formula, the refinement module takes the dehazing output and its per-pixel channel mean to predict a cleaner final image. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

Conceptually, this means the authors observed that **haze removal alone is not enough**. You may get a mostly dehazed image, but still have:

*   color deviations,
*   weak texture recovery,
*   insufficient visual vividness. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

So they introduced a second, lightweight stage specifically to polish those aspects.

# 7) Training / optimization strategy of Baseline 2

Baseline 2 uses a **two-stage training process**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

## Stage I: Train the dehazing module

The refinement module is ignored at first. The dehazing module and discriminator are trained using:

*   **Adam** optimizer,
*   $$\beta_1 = 0.9$$, $$\beta_2 = 0.999$$,
*   initial learning rate **1e-4**,
*   total **5000 epochs**,
*   learning rate decay by **0.5×** at **1500, 3000, and 4000 epochs**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

The Stage I loss is:

*   **L1 loss**,
*   **MS-SSIM**,
*   **perceptual loss**,
*   **adversarial loss**,

with weights:

*   $$\alpha = 0.4$$,
*   $$\beta = 0.01$$,
*   $$\gamma = 0.0005$$. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

## Stage II: Train refinement

Then they train:

*   the refinement module for **500 epochs**,
*   and the whole model for another **500 epochs**,
*   with a fixed learning rate of **1e-5**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

They also explicitly say they **remove the adversarial loss** when optimizing the refinement module. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)

That is a meaningful change from Baseline 1: Baseline 1 uses a single overall objective for the whole model, while Baseline 2 has a **staged optimization plan** because the architecture itself is now functionally separated into **dehazing first, refinement second**. [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf), [\[Baseline2 \| PDF\]](https://urbanandmainlines-my.sharepoint.com/personal/bart_kowal_urbanandmainlines_com/Documents/Microsoft%20Copilot%20Chat%20Files/Baseline2.pdf)
